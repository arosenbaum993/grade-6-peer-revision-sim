ScenarioYou are a student reporter for your school newspaper. Research whether young people should take action to protect the environment and write an article convincing other students why it matters.
Source 1: "What Is Climate Change — and Why It Matters"
Informational Text

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in the Earth's average weather patterns — including temperature, rainfall, seasons, and other climate conditions. Unlike weather (which changes day to day), climate describes what is typical over decades or more.

Scientists have observed that the Earth is warming. Data collected by satellites, weather stations, ice measurements, and ocean monitoring all show increases in global average temperature, melting glaciers and sea ice, rising sea levels, and changes in precipitation patterns.

A major reason for this change is the increase of "greenhouse gases" in Earth's atmosphere — gases like carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide (N₂O), and certain industrial gases. These gases act like a blanket around the Earth: when sunlight reaches Earth it warms the surface, and normally some of that heat would radiate back to space. But greenhouse gases absorb and trap part of the heat, preventing it from escaping, which keeps the planet warmer.

Human activities over the past 150–200 years — especially since the Industrial Revolution — have greatly increased emissions of greenhouse gases. Because of this extra heat, the Earth's climate is changing faster than natural cycles would, with serious consequences including stronger storms, rising sea levels, and threats to wildlife and human health.

Source 2: "Why Reducing Waste and Recycling Helps Our Planet"
Informational Text

Every day, people use lots of materials — paper, plastic, metal, glass — and many of these become waste when we throw them away. But recycling and reducing waste can make a big difference. When we recycle, we reuse materials instead of extracting new ones, conserving natural resources and protecting forests, water supplies, and minerals.

Recycling also reduces greenhouse-gas emissions. The process of creating new products from virgin raw materials often consumes lots of energy and releases carbon dioxide. By contrast, recycling usually uses less energy — which helps lower pollution and slow down climate change.

Finally, recycling supports a more sustainable way of living: a "circular economy," where materials are reused rather than wasted. This not only helps the environment but can also support jobs in recycling and manufacturing, and encourage responsible use of resources so future generations can continue to live on a healthy planet.

Model Essay — Student Reporter Response
Argumentative Writing Sample

As our world changes, it is more important than ever for young people — and especially students — to take action to protect the environment. Climate change is not a distant problem for adults only; it affects all of us, and we have the power to help.

According to environmental scientists, rising global temperatures are caused largely by human emissions of greenhouse gases from burning fossil fuels and other harmful activities. (Source 1) This warming leads to severe weather, rising sea levels, and threats to wildlife and human health. If we do nothing, future generations may face much worse conditions.

But we don't have to wait for someone else to fix things. Simple everyday actions can make a real difference. Recycling paper, plastic, and glass; reducing waste by reusing items; and conserving energy by turning off lights when not in use are effective steps that everyone can do — even students. (Source 2) Families who recycle, reduce waste, and conserve energy help lower pollution and lessen environmental impact.

Moreover, planting trees, using less energy at home, and choosing more eco-friendly transportation — like walking or biking when possible — are all practical ways we can help right now. By doing these things, we show we care about our planet — and we help make it healthier for everyone.

Because we all share this Earth, students and families should act now. Even small steps — recycling, conserving energy, planting trees — add up to big changes over time. If each of us does our part, we can help prevent worse effects of climate change, protect wildlife, and secure a safer, cleaner future. Let's make a difference — starting today.

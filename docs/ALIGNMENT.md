## 2. Task model

The Peer Revision Guidance is explicit that the PRT works from **a peer's draft text**: students "read the draft text and provide helpful feedback aligned to the rubric criterion," and the state's sample stems are overwhelmingly *Which sentence should be added / revised / removed…*

Accordingly the stimulus is a deliberately flawed Grade 6 draft — "Our School Needs Recycling" by a fictional student, Marcus — with **21 numbered sentences across 5 numbered paragraphs**, plus two sources. Numbering lets every item anchor to specific text the way real PRT items do.

### Engineered flaws in the draft

Each flaw maps to a rubric bullet, so the item bank is diagnostic rather than incidental.

| Sentence(s) | Engineered flaw | Rubric bullet |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Imprecise wording ("a thing that a lot of people talk about") | T1 style / precise vocabulary |
| 1–2 | No hook; filler sentence | T1 "Provides a strong introduction" |
| 3 | Hedged, unclear claim ("should probably think about") | T1 "clearly establishes a claim" |
| 4 | Vague quantity where a source statistic exists | T2 "relevant and well-chosen evidence" |
| 5–6 | Two short, choppy simple sentences | T3 "variety of sentence structures" |
| 7 | Vague pronoun reference ("They say"); uncredited source | T3 usage · T2 "attempt to credit sources" |
| 8 | Weak, unelaborated closing to the paragraph | T2 "Elaborates effectively" |
| 11 | Verb tense shift ("collect … sold") | T3 "mostly correct verb tense" |
| 12 | Subject-verb disagreement ("students … was") | T3 usage and conventions |
| 13 | Irrelevant sentence (cousin's school lunches) | T1 "logically grouping ideas" |
| 14 | Subject-verb disagreement ("Programs … saves") | T3 usage and conventions |
| 15 | Weak, generic transition ("Also") | T1 "Uses transition words effectively" |
| 16 | Missing commas in a series | T3 "few, if any, errors in conventions" |
| 17 | Passive voice | T3 "mostly active voice" |
| 18 | Slang and contractions ("kinda," "no-brainer") | T1 "style appropriate for purpose and audience" |
| 19 | Attacks the opposition instead of addressing a counterclaim | T2 "Elaborates … using techniques" |
| 20–21 | Weak conclusion; no call to action | T1 "Provides a strong conclusion" |

**No counterclaim anywhere in the draft.** That is deliberate and internally consistent: item 14 asks students to supply one, and Source 2 contains a genuine opposing view (Dr. Ruiz on contamination) for them to work from.

## 4. Item-writing rules applied

- **Exactly one defensible key per selected-response item.** Stems that could support two answers were rewritten or cut.
- **No length cues.** Every item was checked so the key is not the longest option by a wide margin — the automated threshold is a 1.35× ratio against the longest distractor. Several distractors were lengthened specifically to satisfy this.
- **Plausible distractors.** Every distractor represents a real Grade 6 misconception (hedging mistaken for a claim, a specific figure with no attribution, a counterclaim named but never answered, a "consistent but wrong tense" revision), not filler.
- **Verbatim quotation.** Any option quoting the draft quotes it exactly. Truncating a quotation can silently introduce a new grammatical error, which then contaminates the item.
- **No cross-item leakage.** No item's stem or options reveal another item's key. This is checked automatically (see §7); two violations found by that check were rewritten — item 8 originally quoted item 5's correct transition verbatim, and item 23 originally named one of item 17's two keyed sentences.
- **No internal contradictions.** No two items assert opposite things about the draft. The absence of a counterclaim, for example, is consistent across items 14, 15, and the drag-and-drop distractor in item 16.
- **One-to-one matching is enforced in the UI**, so a matching item cannot be answered by assigning one column to every row.
- **Partial credit on every 2-point item**, mirroring how Georgia Milestones scores 2-point technology-enhanced items.

Structural invariants are checked by an automated audit (see §7). If you edit the bank, re-run it.

## 5. Reliability and score interpretation

**What this form can support**

- Class-level and small-group instructional decisions.
- Item-level misconception analysis (the *most common wrong answer* column is the highest-value cell in the workbook).
- Relative comparison of the three traits within a class.

**What it cannot support**

- A Georgia Milestones achievement-level prediction. The bands are teacher-set practice thresholds. The ALD document states the levels for this assessment are "subject to revisions during the standard setting process that occurs after the first administration," so no percentage-to-level mapping exists to use.
- A high-stakes individual placement decision from trait subscores alone. Each trait rests on 9–11 points; the standard error at that length is wide. The workbook says so in-sheet, and points teachers to item-level evidence to confirm a pattern.
- Stable discrimination statistics below roughly 20 students. The Item Analysis tab flags this automatically rather than displaying a misleadingly precise number.

**Vocabulary flags** are self-reported and therefore asymmetric: they capture words students *know* they do not know. Pair them with item difficulty to find the words students did not realize were blocking them.

## 6. What changed from the previous version

The prior build was a 20-item, 20-point analysis quiz about a *polished model essay*.

| Area | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Task model | Analyze a good essay | Revise a flawed peer draft (matches the PRT) |
| Trait 3 coverage | **0 items** on conventions | 9 points across 7 items |
| Reporting categories | Included "Argumentative," which is not a blueprint category; merged Grammar and Vocabulary | 3 rubric traits **and** 5 blueprint categories, reported separately |
| DOK 3 | 30% as coded, ~15% as written — below the 35% floor | 40% ✓ |
| Scoring | All items 1 point, all-or-nothing | 16×1pt + 7×2pt with partial credit |
| Achievement levels | "Proficient Learner" at 70%, no caveat | Same Milestones level names, but with an explicit statement that the cut points are teacher-set and do not predict a Milestones level |
| Answer key exposure | n/a (no review existed) | Students see per-item points but not the key or rationale; teacher-controlled via `CONFIG.REVIEW` |
| Answer review | None | Every item with response, key, and rationale |

**Item defects corrected.** Internal contradictions between the old Q17 and Q19 (whether the essay contained a counterclaim) and between the old Q6 and Q11 (whether an informal sentence was the *best* conclusion); a double-keyed conclusion item; a matching item with no 1:1 enforcement; a length-cued drop-down; a duplicate item pair split across two different reporting categories; a "which word from Source 2" item answerable by scanning for the only option word present in the source; and two options silently truncated from the passage, one of which introduced a subject-verb agreement error into a non-key.

**Code defects corrected.** Answers locked permanently after one click; `unmarkAnswered` was a no-op in both branches so progress never decremented; multi-select silently discarded the oldest choice at the cap; double submission (hidden-form POST *and* a `fetch`) that logged two rows per student; a `no-cors` fetch whose `.then()` always resolved, reporting success even on failure; drag-and-drop that could not work on touch devices; no persistence, so a refresh destroyed all work; and hard-coded item counts.

## 7. Verification performed

Automated checks run against the built file, not against intent:

- **Structural audit** — one key per SR item; `correctCount` matches keyed options; matching items are square with distinct per-row keys; drag-and-drop banks are at least as large as their slot count; every item carries a trait, standard group, standard, DOK, and rationale.
- **Length-cue scan** — flags any item where the key is ≥1.35× the longest distractor.
- **Cross-item leakage scan** — compares every item's key text against everything a student can read on every other item, flagging substantial verbatim overlap.
- **Answer-key exposure test** — a browser run with every item answered incorrectly, asserting that no key text, and no correct row→column or slot→item pairing, appears anywhere on the results screen.
- **Blueprint math** — DOK distribution by points recomputed from the bank and checked against the blueprint ranges.
- **Browser end-to-end (Chromium)** — all-correct run scores 30/30; all-wrong run scores 0/30; partial credit verified on all seven 2-point items across every 2-point type; matching 1:1 exclusivity holds when a student tries to assign one column to every row; the multi-select cap refuses a third selection instead of silently dropping one; progress decrements when an answer is removed; autosave and resume survive a page reload; tap-to-place works under touch emulation at 390px with no horizontal page overflow.
- **Reporting pipeline** — 25 synthetic submissions with a realistic ability spread were pushed through the Apps Script in a mocked Sheets environment: 25 submission rows, 575 item rows, all ten tabs populated, duplicate submission correctly rejected as idempotent.

To re-run the structural and blueprint checks after editing the bank, extract the `QUESTIONS` array and assert the invariants in §4; the browser tests require Playwright against `index.html`.
